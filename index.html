Early Life & Origins

Born on September 18, 1966, at Ibadan Street West, Ebute Metta, Lagos, to the late David Oladapo Fatukasi and the late Mrs. Florence Olawunmi Fatukasi (née Oginni), Kehinde Olaoluwa Oladapo marks his sixtieth year with a legacy defined by missionary vision, indigenous leadership development, discipleship and institution-building.

His journey reflects a sustained commitment to the Great Commission: reaching communities, raising indigenous workers, strengthening the Church and preparing successive generations for Christian service.

Roots & Pioneer Field Evangelism

Raised among the Awori people of Surulere, Lagos, Oladapo developed an early appreciation of the relationship between culture, community and evangelism. This understanding informed his outreach among the Awori of Igando and his subsequent field evangelism during the 1991–1992 National Youth Service Corps (NYSC) programme in Oyi Local Government Area of the then Anambra State.

Working from Nteje, he ministered faithfully in Nando, Igbariam, Ekulobia and neighbouring communities.

Missionary Formation & Efon Alaaye Training Centre

His formal missionary formation continued at World Outreach School of Missions, Onireke, Ibadan, between 1995 and 1996. On October 4, 1996, he relocated to Efon Alaaye, Ekiti State, where he established a pioneering training centre dedicated to raising indigenous local missionaries.

Drawing students from Ipole-Iloro, Ikogosi, Erinjiyan, Igbara-Odo, Akure and surrounding communities, the centre employed Yoruba as a principal language of instruction, making missionary formation accessible within local linguistic and cultural contexts.

"Its underlying philosophy was unequivocal: indigenous believers must be equipped to evangelise, disciple and serve their own people."

Household of Missions Inc. & International Reach

This vision found broader institutional expression through Household of Missions Inc., a para-church missionary agency committed to strengthening the local Church through evangelism, missionary mobilisation, leadership formation, intercession and cross-cultural engagement.

Beyond Ekiti, the work extended to Benue State, including among the Tiv people, and through strategic partnerships across Nigeria and West Africa. His international ministry engagements have included the Republic of Benin, Togo, Ghana, Côte d’Ivoire, Canada and the United States (including New York, Chicago, Ohio, and New Jersey).

His 2026 West African missionary journey underscores the continuing, uninterrupted reach of this divine mandate.

Educational & Training Legacy

Oladapo's commitment to generational formation also encompasses Christian education. He pioneered Tender Care Christian Academy and HOM High School in Ekiti State, advancing an educational philosophy that integrates academic excellence, biblical values, character formation and leadership development.

His wider training legacy includes the Global Missions Discipleship Panorama (GMDP), a structured curriculum spanning missionary calling, local and global missions, diaspora engagement, cross-cultural communication, evangelism, spiritual formation, theology and ministerial leadership.

The Gatekeepers Apostolic Network complements this work through intercession, spiritual vigilance and territorial responsibility. Missionary retreats, leadership camps, mobile training initiatives and discipleship programmes further translate these convictions into sustained practice.

Academic Foundations & Family Life

His academic and professional foundation includes a B.Sc. in Sociology and Anthropology from Obafemi Awolowo University, Ile-Ife; postgraduate qualifications in Education Planning and Management, including a Postgraduate Diploma and Master of Education; and additional professional training in Human Resource Management, Personnel Management, Health, Safety and Environment (HSE), and Quality Assurance/Quality Control (QA/QC).

A husband of one wife and father of four children—two daughters and two sons—Oladapo celebrates sixty years of life and thirty-two years of missionary leadership with profound gratitude to God.

The Continuing Journey

"His enduring legacy lies not merely in geographical reach, institutions established or resources produced, but in the deliberate formation of disciples, workers and leaders equipped to multiply the work of the Kingdom. Sixty years of grace. Thirty-two years of missionary leadership. One continuing mandate: to disciple, equip, mobilise and send. The journey continues. To God be the glory."
